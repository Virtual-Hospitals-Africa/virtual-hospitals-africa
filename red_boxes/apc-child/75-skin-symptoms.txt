Give urgent attention to the child with skin symptom/s and any of:
• Sudden onset of generalised itch/rash or face/tongue itch/swelling and any of: tight throat/hoarseness, difficulty breathing, wheeze, shock1, dizziness/
collapse, abdominal pain/nausea/vomiting/diarrhoea or exposure to possible allergen2, anaphylaxis likely 37
• Purple/red rash that does not disappear when pressure is applied with any of: neck stiffness, drowsy/confused, temperature ≥ 38°C, headache, nausea/
vomiting: meningococcal disease likely
• Baby < 1 month old with red, swollen skin around umbilical area and/or draining pus
• Weeping skin lesions in child with severe wasting (weight-for-length/height below -3 line), BMI3 below -3 line if ≥ 5 years, or MUAC4 < 11.5cm if < 5
years: severe acute malnutrition with complications likely 126
• Diffuse rash appearing within 3 months of starting a new medication and any of the following, serious drug reaction likely:
 - Temperature ≥ 38⁰C
 - Shock1
 - Jaundice
 - Swollen lymph nodes
Manage and refer urgently:
• Assess and manage child's fluid needs  26. Keep child warm.
• If meningococcal disease likely: give ceftriaxone5 100mg/kg IV/IM  159:12. Avoid injecting ≥ 1g IM at one injection site.
 - Notify6 as suspected meningococcal disease. Isolate if possible, but ensure child can be safely monitored. Use respiratory droplet precautions.
 - Prevent disease in close contacts (household members, school/childcare contacts or anyone directly exposed to child's oral secretions: kissing, mouth-to-
mouth resuscitation, intubation):
• If < 6 years, give ceftriaxone5 125mg IM.
• If 6-12 years, give single dose ciprofloxacin 250mg.
• If > 12 years, give single dose ciprofloxacin 500mg. If pregnant, give instead single dose ceftriaxone5 250mg IM.
