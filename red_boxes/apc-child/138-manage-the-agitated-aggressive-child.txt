Manage the agitated/aggressive child:
• Try to first calm the agitated/aggressive child without restraints or sedation:
 - Ensure the safety of yourself, the child and those around you: ensure security personnel present, call police if needed. They should disarm child if s/he has a weapon.
 - Move to a quieter area/room: reduce noise, dim lights, close curtains, minimise visitors.
 - Introduce yourself and try to verbally calm the child:
• Avoid direct eye contact, sudden movements or approaching child from behind. Stand at least two arm's lengths away. Ensure exit is not blocked.
• Use an honest, non-threatening manner. Use simple language. Avoid talking down, arguing or commanding child to calm down. Explain each step and what is to come next.
• Discuss restraint and offer reward for calmer behaviour.
• Ask about hunger, thirst, pain or discomfort and if needed, address these.
• Listen to child, identify his/her feelings and desires and offer choices. Take all threats seriously.
• Only if verbal attempts to calm the child fail and child is a danger to self or others, consider restraints or sedation:
 - Before restraint/s or sedation:
• If not yet done, assess and manage possible causes of abnormal thoughts or behaviour  136.
• If child < 12 years old, avoid restraints: discuss urgently with family physician, paediatrician or psychiatrist.
• If restraints used, document type, duration and site and check restraint sites every 15 minutes. Counsel the family/caregiver regarding reasons for restraints.
• If sedation needed, discuss with paediatrician or psychiatrist.
• Refer urgently to district psychiatrist at District hospital. Document history and time and dose of each medication given.
