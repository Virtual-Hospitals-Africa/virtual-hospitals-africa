Give urgent attention to the child with eye/vision problems and any of:
• Baby < 1 month old with pus in eyes or swollen eyelids, conjunctivitis of the newborn likely
• Penetrating injury (with/without foreign body)
• Whole eyelid swollen/red or bulging eye, orbital/periorbital cellulitis likely
• Sudden onset redness and excessive eye discharge, with history of sexual contact/
abuse  144, gonococcal conjunctivitis likely
• Shingles involving eye or nose
• One painful red eye
• Corneal ulcer or hazy cornea
• Sudden onset/new squint
• Sudden loss/change in vision (blurred/reduced)
• Sudden drooping of eyelid
• Eyelid laceration
• Chemical burn
• Jaundice 61
Manage and refer urgently:
• If conjunctivitis of newborn likely, give ceftriaxone1 50mg/kg (up to 2g) IM2. Irrigate with sodium chloride 0.9% hourly until referral. Give postnatal care to mother and her partner/s PACK Adult.
• If gonococcal conjunctivitis likely: If < 12 years and < 45kg, give ceftriaxone1 125mg IM. If > 12 years or ≥ 45kg, give ceftriaxone1 250mg IM. If > 12 years also give azithromycin 1g orally.
• If orbital/periorbital cellulitis likely and > 6 hours delay in referral expected, give ceftriaxone1 80mg/kg IM  159:11. Avoid injecting ≥ 1g IM at one injection site.
• If penetrating/metallic foreign body in eye, do not try to remove. Cover gently with protective shield and avoid lying flat.
• If chemical burn: wash eye/s continuously for at least 30 minutes with 1-2 litres of sodium chloride 0.9% or sterile water.
