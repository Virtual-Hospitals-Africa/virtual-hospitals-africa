Reviewer's guide: APC 2026 rule review pages

These pages let a person check, page by page, the VHA rules that AI agents wrote from the APC 2026 guide. This guide explains every part of them. Each "?" on the pages links to its section here.

No clinician has reviewed these rules yet. The checkers' verdicts on the pages are judgments by AI models, not clinical sign-off. Your decisions are what count.

How to review a page

  1. Open a page from the list of pages. Pages with high-priority items are the place to start.
  2. At the top, enter your name as reviewer and choose your role.
  3. Read the red box across the top, its text piece by piece on the left, and the rules on the right. Click any piece of page text, or any condition, to highlight what it is linked to on the other side.
  4. Decide every condition and every red-box element with the buttons under it. Use the note box to say what should change.
  5. The Review queue tab at the bottom lists the items the checkers flagged, high priority first. Click one to jump to it.
  6. Click Export decisions and send the file to Tony.

Your decisions are saved in this browser as you go, so you can stop and come back. They reach nobody until you export them.

The list of pages

The list has one row for each page of the APC 2026 e-book that has a clinical red box: 83 pages. Click a column heading to sort by it; type in the box to filter.

Page

The page number in the APC 2026 e-book.

Title

The page's title. Click it to open the page's review page. "flowchart" after the title means the page has a flowchart, written out in the Flowchart tab.

Change since APC 2023

Whether this page's red box changed between the 2023 and 2026 editions of the guide: changed, unchanged, numbers, wording or position only, or new in 2026. The grouping comes from a script that compared the two editions' pages, so treat it as a rough guide. A page whose box looks unchanged may still differ in a footnote, such as the 2026 definition of shock.

Checks

Whether the page's final rules pass the automated gate, a script with no AI in it. The gate checks that VHA's parser accepts the rules, that every SNOMED concept exists in the 2025-12-01 release, that the rules fit VHA's database, that every condition cites a piece of page text with a verbatim quote, and that the rules meet our reading of Will's six constraints. A pass says the rules are well formed and traceable, not that they are clinically right.

Conditions

How many conditions the page's rules contain. A condition is the smallest testable part of a rule: one finding, test result or time comparison, such as "chest pain", "no fever" or "onset at least 10 minutes ago".

Page elements

How many pieces of page text were extracted from the page: each bullet, line of a box, or flowchart step. Some sit inside the red box and some outside it. The checkers judged each one: is it something the rules should encode, and if so, do they?

Builder agreement

Each page was built twice, by two AI agents working separately from the same instructions. Agreement is how alike the two builds were, from 0 (nothing in common) to 1 (identical): twice the number of conditions they share, divided by their total conditions. Low agreement means the page can be read more than one way, so the reconciler's choices on it deserve a closer look.

Queue

How many items on the page are in its review queue. See Review queue tab.

High

How many of the page's queue items are high priority. See Priority.

Decided here

How many items on the page you have decided in this browser. If the rules change after you decide, your decision is kept but marked "reconfirm", and is counted separately.

Page header

Review queue

How many items on this page are in the review queue, listed in the Review queue tab.

Decided

How many of the page's conditions and red-box elements you have decided, out of all of them. Every condition and red-box element needs a person's decision, not only the queued ones: the queue sets the order, not the limit.

APC 2023 page

The page in the 2023 edition of the guide that covers the same topic, and whether this page's red box changed since then. Details are in the Edition context tab.

Reviewer

Your name, saved with every decision you make and in the exported file. Enter it before deciding anything; it applies across all pages in this browser.

Role

Your role: clinical, engineering or other. It is saved with every decision. Only decisions made with the clinical role count as clinical sign-off, so choose it before you start.

Export decisions

Saves your decisions and notes for this page to a JSON file named after the page and your name. Send it to Tony, who adds it to the shared record of decisions (review/decisions.csv). Export each page you work on.

The two panes

The red box

A band across the top of each page shows the red box cropped from the e-book page, as wide as the window allows. The band takes about a third of the window's height; scroll inside it to see the rest of the box, or drag its bottom-right corner to make it taller or shorter. Click a spot on it to highlight the matching text and conditions. Click the band's title bar to hide it when you need more room for the panes. The page remembers the height and whether the band is hidden. zoom to full size shows the crop at the page's own resolution. hide details, at the top right, does the same for the page header.

Red box text, piece by piece

The left pane lists the red box's text one piece at a time: each bullet, line or flowchart step. The text was extracted from the e-book automatically, so check it against the red box above. Click a piece to highlight the conditions that cite it.

Below the red box text, the pane may list page text from outside the box that a rule cites, or that a checker thinks the rules should have covered.

Rules, in plain English

The right pane shows the page's rules, written out in English from the rule file. Each rule is one of three kinds: a task (what to check or measure when something is found), a diagnosis rule (what is probably going on), or a priority evaluation (how urgent the patient is). Under each condition are small labels showing where it came from and what the checkers said.

Structured rules

show structured rules switches the right pane from English to the rules as written in the rule file, in VHA's Lisp format, indented and colored the way a code editor such as VS Code shows them. Each rule shows its full text exactly as in the file, with the comments above it. Each condition shows its own expression in the longer form VHA uses internally: (clinical_finding X) in the file appears as (finding (snomed_concept "Clinical finding" "finding") X), which means the same thing. The labels and decision buttons stay the same. show English switches back.

The small labels

  • quote: the page words this condition cites. A script checked that the quote appears word for word in the cited text.
  • builder: high / medium / low: how confident the builder was in this condition.
  • checker 1, checker 2, checker 3: the verdicts of three AI checkers. Under a piece of page text (left pane), they are the three page-to-rules checkers, which read the page and judged whether the rules encode that text: correct, partial, missing or error, or out of scope. Under a condition (right pane), they are three different rules-to-page checkers, which read the rules and judged whether each condition is faithful to the page: faithful, imprecise, wrong or unsupported. Each set ran on three different AI models. Green is good, amber a partial objection, red a serious one. Hover a label to see which kind of checker it is.
  • builds differed: the two builds disagreed here, and the reconciler chose. Hover to see the choice. "unsettled" means the reconciler wasn't sure.
  • A SNOMED concept, such as "Chest pain (finding) · 120 descendants": the concept the condition uses, and how many narrower concepts it also matches. VHA's rules match through SNOMED's hierarchy, so a broad concept fires for many specific findings. A red label means the concept doesn't exist in VHA's SNOMED release, and accept is disabled.
  • cited by N conditions, on page text: how many conditions cite it. not encoded: the builder chose not to encode it, with a reason category such as management_step. not accounted for: red-box text that no condition cites and the builder didn't explain.

Deciding

  • For a condition, or page text the rules encode: accept (it's right), fix (it needs changing; say how in the note), or question (you're not sure; say why).
  • For red-box text the rules don't encode: confirm out of scope (rightly left out), should be encoded, or question.
  • Click a chosen button again to undo it. Notes are saved as you type.
  • If the rules change after you decide, the decision shows "rule changed since this decision: reconfirm".

Parts of a rule

When

What must be true for the rule to apply. all of means every condition below must hold, any of means at least one, and none of means none may.

Concludes

For a diagnosis rule: the diagnosis it suggests (usually as "probable") when its conditions hold.

Then

For a task: what the health worker is asked to do once it applies, such as check for a list of findings or take a measurement.

The tabs

The tabs sit at the bottom of each page, below both panes.

Review queue tab

Every item on the page that a person should look at first, high priority first. Click an item to jump to it in the panes, where you decide it. Rule-level problems, builder disagreements and known issues have no buttons of their own; record your view in the note on a condition of the rule concerned.

Scope decisions tab

Every piece of red-box text the rules don't encode, with the builder's reason and the page-to-rules checkers' view of whether it belongs in the rules. Most are management steps (drugs, doses, referral wording), which this project leaves out on purpose. Confirm each, or say it should be encoded.

Builder disagreements tab

Every place where the two builds of the page differed, what the reconciler chose, and why. The figure above the table is the builder agreement.

Edition context tab

This page's APC 2023 counterpart and how its red box changed, and how the 2026 rules compare with Will's 2023 rules for it, where there are any. Most of the 2023 rules are on Will's own not-yet-reviewed list, so a mismatch doesn't say which side is right. It is a comparison of agreement, not a grade.

Remaining questions tab

The builder's notes on the page: criteria the rule language can't hold (for example, lab values such as Hb), places where the rules fire more or less often than the page intends, decisions it made, and questions for a clinician. Read this before deciding the page.

Flowchart tab

The builder's written version of the page's logic, made before the rules so the rules follow the page. It cites the e-book page it comes from.

Checker summaries tab

Each of the six checkers' overall summary of the page, and any problems they found with whole rules (rather than single conditions).

Raw rule file tab

The page's rule file exactly as it would go into VHA, in VHA's Lisp format.

Table columns

In the Review queue tab

Priority

  • High: a checker said page text is missing or wrong in the rules, said a condition is wrong or unsupported by the page, or reported a serious problem with a whole rule; or a checker's report on a condition is missing.
  • Medium: two checkers raised a partial objection; a checker reported a lesser rule problem; the builders' disagreement wasn't settled; a builder declared a known issue; or a rule was repaired after the checkers read it.
  • Low: one checker's partial objection, or a builder disagreement the reconciler settled.

Kind

claim: a condition in the rules. element: a piece of page text. rule: a problem with a whole rule. build_disagreement: a difference between the two builds. known_issue: something a builder declared it couldn't satisfy, with a reason. post_check_repair: a change a script made after the checkers had read the rules.

Item

The text of the condition, page text or rule, with its reference underneath. Click it to jump to it in the panes.

What they said

Each checker's or builder's verdict and reason for this item, and any fix a checker suggested.

In the Builder disagreements tab

Role

Where in a rule the difference sits: trigger (what starts a task), check_for (what a task asks about), priority_condition, diagnosis_condition, diagnosis, or measured (an item a task measures).

Difference

The expression one build had and the other didn't, or wrote differently.

Choice

What the reconciler kept: A, B, both, neither, or new (something different from both). "unsettled" means it wasn't sure.

Reason

The reconciler's reason for its choice.

In the Edition context tab

Role

Where in a rule the conditions sit, as in the Builder disagreements tab.

Only in 2023 rules

Conditions in Will's 2023 rules for this page with no exact match in the 2026 rules. Some differ only in a qualifier or in wording; some reflect changes between editions.

Only in 2026 build

Conditions in the 2026 rules with no exact match in Will's 2023 rules.

Related

Pairs where one side's main SNOMED concept is a narrower or broader version of the other's: "2023 narrower" or "2026 narrower".

How the rules were made

In short: two AI agents built each page separately, following Will's PR #1310 briefing. A third agent settled their differences. A script (the gate) checked the result, and six AI checkers judged it, three from the page to the rules and three from the rules to the page. A script then turned their verdicts into each page's review queue.

The full account, with every figure, limit and what Will's own test made of the rules, is in the run report, RUN-REPORT.md in the folder above this one. Every method choice is in DECISIONS.md.
